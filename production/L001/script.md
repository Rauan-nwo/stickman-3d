## 0. The Office (0:00–0:20)

[SCENE: moneyman's office, A. Green desk lamp, rain on the window. On the desk: an Nvidia box and a brass key. Host leans in, taps the box, then lifts the key into the light.]

HOST: Evening. Mind the box, it's expensive.
HOST: Inside, the most wanted chips on Earth.
HOST: And this key opened every door to them.
HOST: Tonight: who sat beside the man holding it.

## 1. A Table in Singapore (0:20–1:00)

[SCENE: Singapore restaurant, A. Candles, a window onto the night skyline. Jensen (leather jacket) on one side, Alice on the other. Glasses raised. Caption cards with names.]

Picture a quiet dinner in Singapore. On one side sits Jensen Huang, the man in the leather jacket who runs Nvidia, the most valuable company on the planet. On the other sits Alice Huang. Same last name, no relation. Her company is one of his biggest customers in Asia.

JENSEN: To the next shipment.
ALICE: To good partners.

[SCENE: rainy street outside the restaurant, B. A parked car, two agents in headphones, a tape recorder turning. Camera dollies from the window to the car.]

Her company buys his chips by the warehouse, the exact kind China is forbidden to have. And someone else is listening. American intelligence has been tapping her calls for more than a year, and has concluded she works for Beijing.

AGENT: She's talking to an American.
AGENT 2: Which American?
AGENT: The one in the jacket.

[SCENE: back inside, A. Slow push-in on Jensen laughing, unaware. Title card "THE MAN WITH THE KEY" builds over black at the end.]

Jensen has no idea.

AGENT 2: Do we warn him?
AGENT: Not tonight.

So how did China get someone into the seat next to the man who controls the world's AI chips? It starts with a decision in Washington. But first, meet the man at the table. His first job in America was scrubbing toilets in Kentucky.

## 2. The Man With the Key (1:00–3:20)

[SCENE: T map, Taiwan to Kentucky, a plane icon crossing the Pacific; then A: airport, a small boy with a suitcase beside his older brother.]

Jensen was born in Taiwan. At nine, his parents sent him and his older brother to relatives in America, who picked a boarding school in rural Kentucky.

AUNT: Oneida Baptist Institute. Very strict.
UNCLE: Strict sounds perfect.

[SCENE: Kentucky dorm, A. Old brick building, a row of sinks, young Jensen with a mop and bucket bigger than him.]

It was strict, all right. It was a school for troubled kids, and the family had chosen it by mistake. The older boys were sent out to do farm work. Jensen, one of the youngest, got the bathrooms. Every bathroom in the dorm. Every single day.

[SCENE: dorm room, A. A huge tattooed teenager with a taped-up hand holds a book upside down; young Jensen turns it the right way. Then a barbell.]

His roommate was seventeen, covered in tattoos and knife scars, and he couldn't read. So they made a trade.

ROOMMATE: You teach me to read.
YOUNG JENSEN: You teach me the bench press.
ROOMMATE: Deal. Reading first.

Decades later he'd put it simply: he used to clean bathrooms, and now he runs a company.

[SCENE: Denny's booth, A. Night, 1993 caption, three young engineers with coffee cups and a napkin sketch of a chip.]

As a teenager he washed dishes at Denny's. In 1993 he walked back into one, this time as a customer. In a booth in San Jose, he and two engineer friends, Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem, planned a company that would build chips for 3D graphics.

WAITRESS: What can I get you?
JENSEN: Coffee, and a new industry.
WAITRESS: Coffee's on the menu.

[SCENE: small Nvidia office, A. A wall calendar with days crossed out, an almost empty piggy bank. T "big number": ONE MONTH.]

They named it Nvidia, and it came very close to dying. When its make-or-break chip finally shipped in 1997, the company had cash for about one more month of salaries.

CHRIS: How long can we last?
JENSEN: One month.
CHRIS: And if the chip flops?
JENSEN: Then we save on lunch.

[SCENE: C full 3D. One cook at one stove plating a banquet slowly; camera pulls back to a giant kitchen of thousands of tiny cooks flipping at once. Then a game explosion on a monitor.]

The chip was a hit. And the trick inside it changed everything. An ordinary processor is like one brilliant cook preparing a banquet plate by plate. Nvidia's chip is like a kitchen with thousands of cooks, each doing one tiny job at exactly the same moment.

[SCENE: investor meeting, A. Suits around a table, a slide that says CUDA, one investor yawning.]

In 2006, Jensen made his biggest bet. He released software called CUDA, so scientists could put all those cooks to work on any kind of math, not just games. Wall Street didn't get it.

INVESTOR: Who exactly is this for?
JENSEN: People who don't know yet.

[SCENE: T board. A neural-network web lights up; then a long line of lab coats stretching out of a door marked NVIDIA.]

For years it looked like an expensive hobby. Then AI turned out to need exactly that math, and every AI lab on Earth wanted Nvidia chips.

CUSTOMER: How soon can you deliver?
SALESMAN: How patient are you?

[SCENE: split screen, A. Left: a Washington office, a phone ringing. Right: a dim Beijing office, a figure in shadow looking at a photo of Jensen. The brass key glints on Jensen's belt.]

Nvidia became the most valuable company in the world, and Jensen became the man with the key. When he talked about who should be allowed to buy them, Washington picked up the phone. So, it turned out, did Beijing.

AIDE: Get me Jensen Huang.
OFFICER: Find out who else calls him.

That made him the one man China most wanted to get close to. Because in October 2022, Washington slammed a door.

## 3. The Locked Door (4:00–5:30)

[SCENE: Washington office at night, A. A heavy door with a padlock, an official on the phone; Jensen at his desk on the other end.]

That autumn, the US government banned sales of Nvidia's most powerful AI chips to China. The worry was simple: chips that train AI can also help an army.

OFFICIAL: Your best chips can't go to China.
JENSEN: Which ones count as best?
OFFICIAL: The ones they want.

[SCENE: Nvidia lab, A. A conveyor of chips getting smaller each time; each one hits a red STOP stamp.]

Nvidia designed slower versions just for China. Washington tightened the rules, and those were blocked too. So Nvidia made an even slower one. In April 2025, that one was blocked as well.

JENSEN: What about this one?
OFFICIAL: Absolutely not.
JENSEN: It's really slow.
OFFICIAL: Still no.

[SCENE: T "big number" 95% shrinking to 0%. Then A: an assistant holding up an empty chart.]

China had been one of Nvidia's biggest markets. In Jensen's own words, Nvidia's share of China's AI chip market went from ninety-five percent to zero.

ASSISTANT: Here's our China business.
JENSEN: I don't see anything.
ASSISTANT: That's the chart.

[SCENE: conference stage, A. Jensen with a microphone under a spotlight, a big screen behind him.]

Jensen didn't hide how he felt. On stage after stage, he argued that the rules were hurting American companies and handing the market to Chinese rivals.

JENSEN: Let American chips compete everywhere.

[SCENE: dim Beijing office, A. Two faceless officers in silhouette over a desk; a photo of Jensen and a padlock on the desk. Black card at the end: "WHY PICK A LOCK?"]

So here was Beijing's problem. China couldn't buy the chips. But it could try to get close to the man who made them, the same man arguing with Washington about who gets to buy them. Why pick a lock when the locksmith takes meetings?

OFFICER: We can't buy his chips.
OFFICER 2: Then become his customer.

And for that, you would need a company.

## 4. The Cover (5:30–6:45)

[SCENE: Singapore business tower, B. A sign above an office door swaps from "7ROAD" to "MEGASPEED". A clerk with a form at a counter.]

In March 2023, a small Singapore company called 7Road International, the overseas arm of a Chinese video game business, got a new name: Megaspeed. Its new managing director was Alice Huang.

CLERK: Previous business?
STAFFER: Video games.
CLERK: New business?
STAFFER: Now, supercomputers.
CLERK: Big change.
STAFFER: Bigger electricity bill.

[SCENE: T map: Singapore pin, lines to Malaysia and Indonesia, a dotted internet line toward China that never crosses the border.]

Megaspeed called itself a cloud company. That's the perfect business for buying chips, because a cloud company needs mountains of them and nobody asks why. The data centers sat in Malaysia and Indonesia. Not one chip had to enter China. Chinese customers could simply rent the computing power over the internet.

[SCENE: A. A neighbor's window at night; a man inside holds a book up to the glass, a kid outside reads with a flashlight and drops a coin in a jar.]

Imagine you're not allowed to own a certain book. So your neighbor buys it, keeps it in his house, and lets you read it through the window, one page at a time, for a fee. The book never leaves his house. You still read every word.

RENTER: Can we buy your chips?
REP: No. You can borrow them.
RENTER: For how long?
REP: By the hour. Or the year.

[SCENE: Megaspeed office, B. A nameplate "MANAGING DIRECTOR" being unscrewed; the same chair stays occupied in silhouette. T "big number": $33 MILLION.]

On paper, renting computing power isn't exporting chips, and Megaspeed has always said it follows the law. After eight months, Alice Huang stepped down as managing director, but by most accounts she kept a leading role.

CLERK: New title for Alice?
STAFFER: Same office, though.

At the end of 2023, the company's filings showed about thirty-three million dollars in assets. Not the kind whose people end up in photos next to the boss of Nvidia. That was about to change.

## 5. The Introduction (6:45–9:00)

[SCENE: Nvidia event hall, A. Crowd with phones up, Jensen posing for selfies, shaking hands down a long line.]

Jensen Huang is not hard to meet. He shakes every hand, poses for every selfie, and treats big customers like regulars at a diner. Nvidia later said meeting customers is simply part of his job, thousands of them a year.

FAN: Can I get a selfie?
JENSEN: Of course.
FAN 2: Can you sign my laptop?
JENSEN: Only if it has our chip.

[SCENE: California event, A. Name badges on a table: "ALICE HUANG — MEGASPEED". Jensen walks past in the background.]

Alice Huang was one of those thousands. In early 2024, she turned up at an Nvidia event in California, representing Megaspeed.

[SCENE: Taipei at night, B. Neon streets, a banner for Computex on a hall; cut to a busy party room.]

In June 2024, Taipei hosted Computex, the giant computer show where Jensen is treated like a rock star. That same month, at a party in the city, a camera flashed. In the photo, standing right beside Jensen, is Alice Huang.

PHOTOGRAPHER: Everyone, squeeze in!
JENSEN: Customers in the front!
PHOTOGRAPHER: Say "GPU"!
CROWD: GPU! GPU!

[SCENE: T board. The photo pins onto an empty investigation board, alone; a red string hangs loose, not yet attached.]

A company later posted the picture online. To everyone in that room, it was one more snapshot from one more busy night. Nobody looked at it twice. Later, a lot of people would.

[SCENE: accounting office, A. Two clerks, an order printer spitting paper onto the floor.]

Because that same month, Megaspeed's sister company in Malaysia started buying. A lot.

ACCOUNTANT: Another order from Malaysia.
ACCOUNTANT 2: Didn't they order yesterday?
ACCOUNTANT: That was the small one.

[SCENE: T "big number": $33 MILLION grows to $3 BILLION. Then A: two bankers at a window.]

By the end of 2024, the little company's assets had jumped to about three billion dollars.

BANKER: Last year, a small firm.
BANKER 2: And this year?
BANKER: Ninety times bigger.
BANKER 2: Did they sell a hit game?

[SCENE: A. A corner bakery with a mountain of flour sacks blocking the door; neighbors peering in.]

If a corner bakery grew like that in a year, the neighbors would start asking about the flour. So where was all that hardware coming from?

## 6. The Pipeline (9:00–10:30)

[SCENE: US office park, B. A clean sign "AIVRES"; a clerk at a desk with a thick blacklist binder.]

Much of it came from a company called Aivres. Aivres is the American arm of Inspur, a Chinese server giant that the United States had put on its trade blacklist. Inspur couldn't buy Nvidia's best chips. Its American branch could, and it built them into servers and shipped them abroad.

CLERK: Inspur? You're blacklisted.
REP: I'm Aivres. California.
CLERK: Then welcome, Aivres.

[SCENE: C full 3D, "follow one box". A crate with the Nvidia logo goes: factory floor → crane at a port → container ship at night → customs gate → truck → Malaysian data center door.]

Follow one box. It's packed in a factory, loaded onto a ship, cleared through customs, and delivered to a data center in Malaysia. Every step has paperwork. Every step looks legal.

CUSTOMS: Destination, please?
DRIVER: Malaysia, sir.
CUSTOMS: Final destination?
DRIVER: Still Malaysia.
CUSTOMS: Enjoy your stay.

[SCENE: T "big number": NEARLY $2 BILLION, then a trade-records ledger scrolling to $4.8 BILLION.]

One count put the haul at nearly two billion dollars of Nvidia's most advanced chips. A later look at trade records found more than four point eight billion dollars of AI equipment with Nvidia inside, all flowing to one company most people had never heard of.

[SCENE: Malaysian data center, B. Long silent aisles stacked with sealed, shrink-wrapped crates; an inspector with a flashlight and a clipboard; a guide in a lanyard.]

Late in 2024, inspectors from the US Commerce Department walked into one of those Malaysian data centers. A data center is supposed to hum with busy servers. What they found were servers still sealed in their packaging.

INSPECTOR: Is it switched on?
GUIDE: It's still wrapped.
INSPECTOR: Since when?
GUIDE: Since delivery.

[SCENE: Nvidia office, A. A tally counter clicking, rows of chip icons ticked off one by one.]

Still, on paper, nothing was wrong. Every sale went to Singapore or Malaysia, never to China. Megaspeed said every review found no violations. Jensen later said Nvidia had studied all its deployments.

JENSEN: We counted every one.

[SCENE: customs gate, A. A nervous smuggler with a bulging suitcase sweats; behind him a calm truck rolls through with a stamped stack of papers. Black card: "THE CHIPS WEREN'T THE PRIZE".]

That's what made it so hard to catch. Ordinary smugglers sweat at customs. This operation never had to. Every box was exactly where the paperwork said it was. But in the eyes of American intelligence, the chips were never the real prize.

## 7. The Real Prize (10:30–12:00)

[SCENE: Washington office, A. A whiteboard; an official keeps erasing and rewriting "BLOCKED / 15% / 25%".]

Nvidia insists its customers never get anything that matters for national security. The prize was influence. Because Washington could not make up its mind. In April 2025, the China chip was blocked, and Nvidia wrote off billions in chips it suddenly couldn't sell. In August came a deal: sales could resume if the government got fifteen percent. In December, the president went further and allowed a stronger chip, for twenty-five percent.

OFFICIAL: China chip: blocked.
JENSEN: Understood. Blocked.
OFFICIAL: Now allowed. Fifteen percent, please.
JENSEN: Wait. Understood?
OFFICIAL: Stronger chip allowed. Bigger cut.
JENSEN: Does anyone have a calendar?

[SCENE: Taipei press room, A. A wall of microphones, flashbulbs.]

Through every twist, Jensen made the same case, loudly. In Taipei that May, he told reporters exactly what he thought of the rules.

JENSEN: The export control was a failure.

He warned that China's AI market was heading toward fifty billion dollars, and American companies were locked out of it.

[SCENE: dim Beijing office, A. A chessboard: a single chip piece on one side, a tiny White House piece on the other; a hand slides past the chip.]

Now look at it from Beijing. One chip is valuable. But one conversation between Jensen and the White House could open or close an entire market. Which would you rather sit next to?

OFFICER: A chip, or a conversation?
OFFICER 2: The conversation. It's cheaper.

[SCENE: T board, red strings connecting MEGASPEED to SENATE, COMMERCE, JUSTICE. Then A: three desks passing a folder down the line.]

Then Megaspeed suddenly went quiet and stopped placing orders. That October, Senator Tom Cotton asked the Commerce Department to investigate it, and Singapore police opened a case of their own. Then the Commerce probe was handed over to the Justice Department.

SENATOR: Look into Megaspeed.
COMMERCE: We're looking.
JUSTICE: We'll take it from here.

And by then, the people listening to Alice Huang's calls had put everything in writing.

## 8. The Memo (12:00–12:45)

[SCENE: T document. A thick file stamped TOP SECRET slides onto a desk; lines of text highlight one by one. Two aides in a secure room.]

In the spring of 2026, a highly classified memo was written under the watch of Secretary of State Marco Rubio. It was built on electronic surveillance of Alice Huang. Its conclusion: China's Ministry of State Security had recruited her to gather information on American AI chips, and to nudge Jensen to keep lobbying for looser rules.

AIDE: Who gets a copy?
AIDE 2: Only the top clearances.
AIDE: Does Jensen get one?
AIDE 2: He's in it.

[SCENE: newsroom, A. A front page printing: NEW YORK TIMES, Oct. 29. Then Jensen on a TV screen in a hallway.]

The memo went around before the president's trip to Beijing in May. Then, on October 29, 2026, the New York Times broke the story. China called it a smear. Jensen said his contacts with her were about business.

JENSEN: Completely normal and not unusual.

[SCENE: newsroom, A. An editor flips a page in the leaked file; a reporter's eyebrows go up.]

In early November, the Times got hold of the memo's details. And the memo said one more thing: the relationship had gone beyond business.

REPORTER: Beyond business?
EDITOR: Read page two.

That left one question that still has no good answer.

## 9. The Empty Chair (12:45–14:00)

[SCENE: suburban front door, A. Two agents in suits knock; a man in slippers opens the door, holding a coffee mug.]

Why did nobody tell Jensen? When American spies find a foreign agent getting close to an American, there's a tool for exactly this. It's called a defensive briefing. Agents quietly explain who your new friend works for.

AGENT: Got a minute?
CITIZEN: Am I in trouble?
AGENT: No. Your new friend is.

[SCENE: Jensen's front door, A. Same door, nobody knocks. The porch light stays on, empty.]

Jensen never got that knock. The people who knew listened to the calls, wrote the memo, and briefed the White House. Nobody told the man at the table.

[SCENE: Nvidia office, A. A laptop screen with an email to staff; Jensen's silhouette at a dark window.]

On November 8, Jensen wrote a letter to his staff. In it, he admitted he had been unfaithful to his wife. He didn't name anyone. The press soon did.

JENSEN: I was unfaithful to my wife.

Then everything moved at once.

BOARD: We're opening an independent investigation.
CONGRESSMAN: We'd like a closed-door briefing.
IMMIGRATION: Alice Huang? Entry denied.

[SCENE: Nvidia headquarters at night, B. Camera drifts down an empty corridor to the CEO's office; the leather chair is empty. T ticker: NVDA −13%.]

On November 12, Jensen Huang resigned as CEO, president, and board member, effective immediately. Thirty-three years at the top, over in one press release. Chief financial officer Colette Kress became interim CEO.

REPORTER: Any reason given?
SPOKESMAN: None at all.

That day, Nvidia's stock fell as much as thirteen percent.

ANALYST: What did the affair cost?
ANALYST 2: About thirteen percent.
ANALYST: Of him?
ANALYST 2: Of your retirement fund.

Nobody has charged Jensen, Nvidia, or Alice Huang with any crime. Where she is now, nobody will say.

[SCENE: the Singapore restaurant from chapter 1, A. Same candles, same glasses, two empty chairs. Slow pull-back through the window to the rainy street; the agents' car is gone.]

Go back to that dinner in Singapore. The candles. The toast to good partners. Both chairs are empty now. America watched that table for more than a year and never said a word to the man sitting at it. So the next time someone friendly pulls up a chair beside the people building America's AI, who's going to warn them?

## 10. The Office (14:00–14:15)

[SCENE: moneyman's office, A. Host turns the brass key in his fingers, opens a small safe, places the key inside, shuts the door. Click. He checks his pocket watch. Lamp off.]

HOST: Some keys are too heavy to carry.
HOST: So this one goes in the safe.
HOST: The question is who opens it next.
